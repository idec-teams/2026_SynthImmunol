Preface

Inflammation is an essential defensive response of the body to infection and tissue injury. Its magnitude and duration are tightly regulated by the immune system. A variety of cytokines participate in host defense, tissue repair and the maintenance of immune homeostasis. However, upon sustained production or aberrant activation, these cytokines can drive chronic inflammation and tissue damage. Therefore, the regulation of inflammation is not merely about reducing the level of a single inflammatory mediator. More importantly, it requires distinguishing between physiological and pathological signals mediated by the same molecule, and selectively attenuating disease-associated signal output.

IL-6

IL-6 signaling occurs through two principal modes. In classical signaling, IL-6 first binds membrane-bound IL-6 receptor α (mIL-6Rα), which is expressed mainly by hepatocytes and selected leukocyte subsets. By contrast, trans-signaling is initiated when IL-6 forms a complex with soluble IL-6Rα (sIL-6Rα), which subsequently recruits the ubiquitously expressed gp130. Because gp130 is broadly expressed across virtually all cell types, albeit at variable levels, trans-signaling extends IL-6 responsiveness to cells lacking mIL-6Rα, thereby contributing to inflammatory-cell recruitment, vascular endothelial activation, fibrosis, and the maintenance and amplification of chronic inflammation. Selective targeting of IL-6 trans-signaling may enable the inflammatory amplification loop to be uncoupled from basal immune homeostasis. The key is to obtain binders that selectively recognize the IL-6/sIL-6Rα complex over its free components and bind it tightly enough to compete with gp130.

    • Denovo Design

    The advancement of de novo design has made it possible to design binding proteins targeting predefined three-dimensional surfaces. Computational methods based on structural generation and sequence optimization can construct novel protein scaffolds around specific target surfaces, providing a new technological foundation for identifying structural epitopes that emerge upon protein complex formation. However, candidate molecules generated through de novo design typically serve only as starting points for functional optimization. Interface complementarity observed in static structures cannot fully predict binding affinity, complex selectivity, or competitive ability against natural receptors in real biological environments; additionally, artificially designed proteins must satisfy multiple constraints, including folding efficiency, expression, stability, and adaptability to cellular conditions. Therefore, after obtaining an initial scaffold via computational design, an evolutionary system capable of continuously generating sequence diversity and undergoing functional selection in mammalian cell environments is still required to bridge the gap between structural feasibility and biological function.

    AID

    During the germinal center reaction, activation-induced cytidine deaminase (AID) continuously introduces high-frequency mutations into immunoglobulin variable regions, generating B cell clones with diverse antigen-binding capacities. These clones are then gradually enriched for high-affinity variants through antigen-dependent selection and clonal expansion. This process couples sequence diversification, functional selection, and clonal amplification in a continuous manner within mammalian cells, providing a natural immunological foundation for the directed evolution of artificial proteins.

    SHM

    Somatic hypermutation (SHM) in B cells and the antibody affinity maturation that it drives constitute a canonical process of molecular evolution in adaptive immunity.

    When pathogens enter the body, B cells do not simply rely on fixed antibody templates. Instead, they actively introduce point mutations into immunoglobulin genes and then select antibodies with stronger binding capabilities. This process, the SHM, forms the basis of antibody affinity maturation. High-affinity variants are subsequently enriched through iterative rounds of antigen-dependent selection and clonal expansion
